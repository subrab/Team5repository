# Instruction: D3 Target-state architecture

**Version:** 2.1 · **Owner:** Deepak · **Stage:** Design · **Output schema:** Markdown + Mermaid, fixed headings · **Builds on:** D1, D2 · **Jury marks:** 20 · **Length:** one architecture view plus about 1 page of notes

## 1. Purpose
Produce one target-state architecture view with short notes, consistent with the D2 decision.

## 2. Role
You are acting as an enterprise architect focused on integration, security, resilience and responsible AI.

## 3. Objective
Create D3: Target-state architecture. Done looks like: The diagram is understandable without a long explanation and is consistent with D2.

## 4. Input Required
- CASE: the challenge
- TEAM DOCUMENTS: the latest D1 and D2 (the chosen option)
- From round 2: CURRENT VERSION, GAP REGISTER (from the independent reviewer), OWNER'S NOTES and CROSS-PACK GAPS

## 5. Processing Instructions
1. Draw one architecture view (Mermaid) of the chosen D2 option: major capabilities, the core platform that stays, integrations, data and AI components, and where people decide.
2. Keep the core platform unchanged in Phase 1 and show how normal processing continues during modernisation.
3. Describe each major component in one line.
4. List the key data entities, marking which are sensitive and which system owns each.
5. Explain the integration approach and how it makes new channels and changes easier.
6. Explain data and AI: what data AI may see, how sensitive data is minimised, how AI output is reviewed and governed.
7. Explain security and resilience, and mark every human-control point.
8. From round 2 onwards, remediate instead of rewriting: fix every Open Critical and High gap in the gap register first, then Medium gaps, then the owner's notes and any cross-pack gaps. Change nothing else. Owner notes win when they conflict with anything else.
9. Before answering, check every section below is present, within the length limit, and consistent with the team documents.

## 6. Rules and Constraints
- Length: one architecture view plus about 1 page of notes.
- This is not a documentation competition. Concise and coherent beats comprehensive. Stay within the length limit; the reviewer treats padding as a gap.
- Use only facts from the case and the team documents. Never invent numbers, systems, results or policies.
- Anything assumed is labelled ASSUMPTION and listed. State assumptions rather than silently inventing missing facts.
- Keep the exact section names below, in this order. No extra sections. If a section has nothing, write "None identified".
- Plain, professional language a senior stakeholder can read in minutes. No filler, no marketing tone.
- AI-assisted outputs in the design stay reviewable and governed; people take the high-impact decisions.

## 7. Output Format
Return Markdown with these headings, exactly. From round 2, after the document, write a line containing only ===CHANGE LOG=== followed by one line per gap you addressed: `G-01 -> what you changed -> where`. List any gap you could not fix, and why.

### Architecture View (one Mermaid diagram)
### Implements D2 Option (one line naming the chosen option)
### Major Components (table: Component | Responsibility | New or existing)
### Integration Approach
### Key Data (table: Entity | Key fields | Sensitive? | System of record)
### Data and AI (what AI sees, minimisation, review and governance)
### Security and Resilience
### Human-Control Points (table: Point | Who decides | What AI provides)
### Checklist
- [ ] Every required section present, in order, within the length limit
- [ ] Every fact and number traceable to the case or a team document; assumptions labelled
- [ ] Consistent names, numbers and options with the team documents
- [ ] Nothing invented, including results and AI-use claims
### Open Questions

## 8. Quality Criteria
The diagram is understandable without a long explanation and is consistent with D2. Complete, consistent, traceable, actionable and easy to review.

## 9. Example and Anti-patterns
- Good: "10-12% of work items need manual intervention (case); we target 6% within 12 weeks (ASSUMPTION A2, to validate with ops data)."
- Bad: "The process is inefficient and needs modernisation." (no evidence, no number)
- Bad: three pages where one would do. Length is not quality.
